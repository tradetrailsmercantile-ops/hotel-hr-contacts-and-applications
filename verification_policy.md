# Finland Job Verification Policy

**Current scope:** Finland only, all regions.  
**Policy last reviewed:** 2026-10-09  
**Search status:** The user explicitly authorized the search on 2026-10-09.

## 1. Locked job categories

Only search and record roles that clearly fit one of these user-approved categories. Do not add countries, categories, or unrelated job titles without the user's approval.

1. Cleaner / Housekeeper
2. General Worker / Labourer
3. Construction Helper
4. Warehouse / Material Handling Helper
5. Production / Factory Helper
6. HOUSEPERSON
7. Construction Cleaner / Labour

A job's wording may differ slightly, but the actual duties must fit one of these categories. Do not use category similarity to bypass explicit job requirements.

## 2. Six verification layers

### Layer 1 — Scope and live-vacancy check
- Confirm the job is in Finland and clearly fits a locked category.
- Record the exact title, town/region, publication date and deadline.
- Confirm that applications are still being accepted.
- Prefer the employer's own careers page or application portal. If a third-party board still shows a job but the employer says it is closed, treat it as closed.
- A generic open application is not the same as a confirmed named vacancy.

### Layer 2 — Employer identity
- Verify the employer's legal name and, where available, Finnish Business ID (Y-tunnus) through reliable company/register information.
- Match the vacancy, contact information and application route against the employer's official website or a recognised job-service listing.
- For an agency/staffing firm, distinguish the staffing company that will employ the worker from the end-client workplace.
- A Gmail address, WhatsApp-only contact, or lack of a professional domain is a reason for further review, not proof of fraud on its own.

### Layer 3 — Vacancy and contract terms
Record or obtain written confirmation of:
- employee's legal employer and worksite;
- employment start/end dates and probationary period;
- contract type and minimum guaranteed hours;
- gross base wage, pay frequency and applicable collective agreement;
- who arranges accommodation, its cost and deductions;
- transport needs, language requirements and mandatory experience/qualifications.

Do not assume full-time work from flexible, casual, on-call or “as needed” wording. Treat unclear minimum hours as REVIEW.

### Layer 4 — Salary and income threshold
For a standard Finnish residence permit for an employed person (TTOL), Migri's published 2026 rule is:
- total gross salary of at least **EUR 1,600 per month**;
- salary must also meet the applicable collective agreement;
- evening/night supplements are excluded from the EUR 1,600 threshold;
- zero-hours/on-demand contracts do not meet the TTOL requirement because regular minimum hours are not agreed;
- part-time income is assessed using working hours × hourly wage × 4.3;
- up to 50% of salary may consist of fringe benefits, valued under the applicable tax rules.

Official source: https://migri.fi/en/working-in-finland/income-requirement  
TTOL details: https://migri.fi/en/residence-permit-for-an-employed-person

**Project screening rule:** Keep EUR 1,600/month as the project's conservative minimum. Do not mark a lead as salary-verified when the wage is hidden, when guaranteed hours are missing, or when the threshold depends on uncertain supplements. Finland has more than one work-based permit route; seasonal-work roles need a separate permit-category check. Never describe EUR 1,600 as a universal threshold for every permit type.

### Layer 5 — Work/residence permit compatibility
- For a non-EU/EEA applicant, confirm the correct residence-permit route for the job and intended dates.
- Ask whether the employer will consider a candidate currently outside Finland and provide the employment terms/information required for the application.
- Do not treat a job as permit-compatible just because the employer is genuine or the vacancy says “English”.
- If an ad explicitly says applicants must already have a Finnish residence/work permit, reject it for a first-time overseas applicant.
- Do not promise sponsorship or permit approval. The competent authorities make the permit decision.

Official starting point: https://migri.fi/en/working-in-finland

### Layer 6 — Fraud and fee screen
Reject or hold a vacancy if any of the following is present:
- fee to obtain the job, offer letter, recruitment slot or a promised permit;
- payment to an individual's personal account, cryptocurrency or gift card;
- “guaranteed visa/permit” claims;
- employer, vacancy or contact cannot be independently linked;
- a copied listing that the real employer says is closed or does not recognise;
- salary appears to meet the threshold only through uncertain bonuses/supplements;
- zero-hours/on-demand terms presented as a reliable TTOL route;
- request for sensitive identity/banking documents before the employer and process have been verified.

Do not label an employer fraudulent solely because a detail is missing. Use REVIEW until evidence supports a decision.

## 3. Required data for every lead

Store the source URL, official employer URL where found, last checked date, status, reason, wage/hours, deadline, application route, accommodation details and permit-compatibility evidence. Clearly mark missing data as unknown or not stated; never fill gaps with guesses. Never invent job leads, contacts, salaries or permits.

## 4. Status definitions

- **VERIFIED** — correct category and Finland location; live vacancy confirmed; employer and application route independently checked; written employment terms establish adequate gross pay, collective-agreement compliance and regular guaranteed hours; permit route and willingness to employ a candidate applying from outside Finland are confirmed; no unresolved scam flags.
- **REVIEW** — plausible, current lead, but at least one material detail (pay, hours, employer connection, permit route, application status or overseas hiring) is unconfirmed.
- **REJECTED** — closed/expired, outside scope, explicit prior-permit requirement for a first-time overseas applicant, contract terms incompatible with the selected permit route, clear salary/eligibility failure, or credible fraud indicators. Record the actual reason; REJECTED does not automatically mean the employer is fraudulent.

## 5. Workflow

Search → Scope check → Employer check → Vacancy/contract check → Salary and collective-agreement check → Permit-route check → Scam/fee check → Record evidence → assign VERIFIED / REVIEW / REJECTED.

Only VERIFIED leads may be presented as having passed every project check. REVIEW leads may be presented as leads to clarify, never as confirmed visa-ready jobs.
